The four-tier marking matrix

A destination address resolves to exactly one tier before the pick list prints. Tiers are cumulative: Tier 2 is the federal marking plus California's additions, and Tier 3 is a separate conjunctive test that a federally compliant unit will fail on its own.

Why this is on a public page

The marking regime is real operating overhead. It is also the reason a grey-market importer cannot service a California or New York retailer at our price without either eating a rework cost or shipping something the retailer cannot legally sell. We publish the matrix because the work is the product.

02

Five federal markings, not one

The most common error in supplier paperwork is treating the orange muzzle plug as the only way to satisfy the federal rule. 16 CFR § 1272.3 permits five distinct constructions. Any one of them is compliant on its own at the federal level, and several of them are cheaper or more durable than a plug on the SKUs we carry.

  1. Blaze-orange muzzle plug — a solid plug of the specified color, permanently affixed in the muzzle end of the barrel, recessed no more than 6 mm.
  2. Blaze-orange barrel band — a ring of the specified color covering the circumference of the barrel at the muzzle end, in place of a plug.
  3. Entire exterior surface in blaze orange — the whole device finished in the specified color rather than a localized marking.
  4. Entire exterior surface in white, bright red, bright orange, bright yellow, bright green, bright blue, bright pink or bright purple — singly, or as the predominant color in combination with other colors in any pattern.
  5. Transparent or translucent construction — the device constructed of materials through which the internal components are visible, which satisfies the rule without any applied marking at all.

03

Do not ship — blocked by ZIP, not by state

Blocked at cart, before payment

New Jersey (statewide) · New York City · Chicago · Philadelphia. These destinations are refused by the cart. There is no marking configuration, no waiver on file and no dealer tier that opens them. A dealer with a valid resale certificate in any of these jurisdictions still cannot receive a gun SKU from us.

The New York State four-part conjunctive test

For destinations in New York State outside the five boroughs, all four of the following must be true of the unit at the same time. This is the test that a federally compliant unit fails, because the federal rule is satisfied by any one of five constructions and New York requires four in combination.

  1. Exterior color or transparency — the entire exterior surface white, bright red, bright orange, bright yellow, bright green, bright blue, bright pink or bright purple, singly or as the predominant color in any pattern; or the device constructed entirely of transparent or translucent materials.
  2. Closed barrel — the barrel closed with the same material the device is constructed from, for a distance of not less than 6 mm from the muzzle end.
  3. Muzzle marking — a blaze-orange or bright-orange marking permanently affixed at the muzzle end.
  4. Full-length barrel stripe — a blaze-orange stripe running the entire length of the barrel on both sides and along the top. This is the requirement that most imported stock does not carry and cannot be retrofitted onto cleanly.

Why ZIP, and why prefix matching is not enough

Three of the four blocked jurisdictions are municipal. A state dropdown cannot express them. A New York State order that satisfies the whole Art. 39-B test is still unlawful the moment the ship-to line reads a Manhattan address, and a Pennsylvania order is fine until the ZIP is a Philadelphia one.

Prefix matching is also insufficient, and this is where most implementations quietly leak. The 110–116 range contains both Queens ZIP codes, which are blocked, and Nassau County ZIP codes, which are not. We match the full five-digit code against a maintained list rather than against a prefix, and the list is reviewed on the same cycle as this page.

Operator note

The block is enforced at cart, not at the warehouse. A dealer never has an order accepted and then reversed, and no card is authorized against a shipment we cannot fulfill. Parts, magazines and accessories that are not themselves imitation firearms are not affected by any of the four tiers.

04

Citation correction: 16 CFR 1272, not 15 CFR 272

The federal marking rule moved. A large share of supplier agreements, distributor terms and compliance annexes still in circulation cite the Department of Commerce rule at 15 CFR Part 272. That rule was removed effective 2026-02-19. Citing it today is citing a rule that no longer exists.

Removed — do not cite

15 CFR Part 272

Department of Commerce. Removed effective 2026-02-19. Any agreement, packing note or dealer annex still pointing here is pointing at nothing, and an indemnity drafted against it is drafted against a repealed obligation.

Controlling

16 CFR Part 1272

Consumer Product Safety Commission. Marking of toy, look-alike and imitation firearms, including the specified color SAE AMS STD 595A-17 no. 12199 and the five permitted constructions in § 1272.3.

Practical consequence for a dealer: if your existing distributor's paperwork cites 15 CFR 272, that paperwork has not been reviewed since at least February 2026. It is a cheap and reliable test of whether a supplier's compliance posture is maintained or inherited.

05

General Certificate of Conformity

A General Certificate of Conformity is the importer's written certification that a product subject to a Consumer Product Safety Commission rule complies with that rule. For imported goods the obligation sits with the importer of record — which is us, not the factory and not the retailer. The certificate must be furnished to distributors and retailers, and must accompany or be available for the shipment.

We issue a GCC per SKU per production lot, we keep the underlying test records, and we furnish the certificate with the shipment rather than on request. A dealer who is audited holds the document already.

Review status and counsel disclaimer

Review

Last reviewed 2026-08-13

Reviewed on a quarterly cycle and out of cycle whenever a cited authority is amended or removed. The 2026-02-19 removal of 15 CFR Part 272 triggered an out-of-cycle review; the correction in section 04 is the result.

Scope

Federal marking rule, four state and municipal tiers, four refused jurisdictions, certificate of conformity content and age of sale. Out of scope: customs classification and duty, state sales-tax nexus, carrier hazmat rules for compressed gas, and any jurisdiction outside the United States.
